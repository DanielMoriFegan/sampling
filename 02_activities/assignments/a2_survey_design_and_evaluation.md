## Part B - Survey Evaluation:

Identify and describe survey features:

```
1. Sample type - Stratified probablity sampling.

2. Sample size - For each province, a minimum sample size was calculated to ensure certain estimates would have acceptable sampling variability at the stratum level. ONce these targets were met, the remaining sample was allocated to the stratta to balance precision on both the national-level and stratum level estimates. A field sample of approximately 50,000 units was used, with an estimated completion of 24,000 questionnaires. 

3. Target population - All people 15 and older living in the 10 provinces of Canada. Excluding full-time residents of institutions. 

4. Sampling frame - Landline and cellular telephone numbers from the Census and administrative sources with Statsitics Canada's dwelling frame. 

5. Survey mode(s) - Survey mode consisted of electronic querstionnaires, or through computer assisted telephone interview. 

6. Timeline - Reference period is the past 12 months preceding the interview date, with collection frequency of every 5 years from September to December. 

7. Response rate - 41.9%

8. Weights - The GSS accounnts for weighting in the survey, where a weighing factor for analysis at the individual level in addition to bootstrap weightrs were utilised for design-based variance estimations. Additionally, the survey data was also weighted to better represent the target poopulation with regards to certain charactersitics so that the characteristics are correlated with independent estiamtes. 

9. Data processing - The survey data underwent data processing in the forms of editing at the macro and micro levels, both automatically and manually at various stages. These included the family, consistency and flow edits to ensure integrity of matrix data, consistency of survey data and fix off-path situations. Additionally, data was processed via imputation, followed by estimation of survey data via weighing and finally quality evaluation was also undergone.  

10. Cleaning, imputation, etc - Imputations were made using donor records selected via a score function. Here, variables with item or partial non-resposne were compared with variable characteristics from the donor records, and when the characterstic was identical for donor records and receipient records, the donor score increased, where the highest donor score was deemed the nearest donor and was chosen to fill in the missing information of the non-respondent. If more than one donor record had the higest score, one was randomly selected. 

Imputation was carired out in 9 steps, which included: imputing personal income and family income, imputing formal vounteering variables in the master file, imputing informal volunteering variables in the master file and then imputing variables in the donation file and solicitation methods in the master file. 

11. Sources of error - Sources of error included sampling error, non-sampling error, non-response bias and coverage error. 
Sampling error arose as these data are based off of sample of perons, resulting in sampling error as each estimate based on a sample will vary from sample to sample. Due to this, bootstrap method using bootstrap weights were used to account for this.
Non-sampling error included imperfect coverage and non-response, where households without telephones or telephone services were not covered by the current frame. 
The sample only included the 10 provinces and had excluded the 3 territories, resulting in coverage error. 

12. Limitations, known biases, etc - As mentioned above, as this was a sample of persons, there is always sampling error. Although bootstrapping methods and bootstrap weights were utilised to address this, there will always be variability. 
Another limitation is that the survey exlcuded the 3 territories, as well as individuals residing in institutions, which can lead to coverage and selection bias. 
There are non-response biases especially as the sampling frame excluded those without telephones or telephone services. 
Another limitation is time-commitment, cost and the potential difficulty in measuring longitudinal time, as the same individuals may not be tracked between each 5 year colleciton point.
Finally, as the survey asks individuals to reflect onthe past 12 months, recolleciton bias may also occur.

```
